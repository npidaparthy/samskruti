[1]
O Aditya (primordial deity), I bow to you. O Bhaskara (illuminator), be gracious unto me. O Divakara (maker of the day), I bow to you. O Prabhakara (creator of light), salutations to you.

[2]
I bow to that Sun god who rides a chariot drawn by seven horses, who is fierce and radiant, who is the son of Kashyapa, and who holds a white lotus.

[3]
I bow to that Sun god who rides a red chariot, who is the progenitor of all worlds, and who is the great remover of sins.

[4]
I bow to that Sun god who embodies the three gunas (sattva, rajas, tamas), who is supremely heroic, who is the embodiment of Brahma, Vishnu and Maheshvara, and who is the great remover of sins.

[5]
I bow to that Sun god who is the dense mass of brilliance, who pervades wind and space, and who is the lord and sustainer of all worlds.

[6]
I bow to that Sun god who resembles the banduka flower in colour, who is adorned with garlands and ear-rings, and who holds a single wheel (the solar disc).

[7]
I bow to that Sun god who is the creator of the universe, whose radiance is immense and illuminating, and who is the great destroyer of sins.

[8]
I bow to that Sun god who is the master of all worlds, who bestows wisdom, knowledge and liberation, and who is the great remover of sins.

[9]
Whoever recites this Suryashtakam daily, all planetary afflictions are destroyed. The childless gain children; the poor become wealthy.

[10]
One who consumes meat and alcohol on a Sunday shall be afflicted with disease for seven births and shall remain in poverty across all those births.

[11]
Those who abstain from oil, honey and meat on Sundays shall be freed from disease, sorrow and poverty, and shall attain the realm of the Sun.
