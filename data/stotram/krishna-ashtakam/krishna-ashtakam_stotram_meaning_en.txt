[1]
I bow to Krishna, the son of Vasudeva, the destroyer of Kamsa and Chanura, the supreme joy of Devaki, and the universal teacher of all worlds.

[2]
I bow to Krishna, who resembles the blue flax flower (atasi) in hue, adorned with garlands and anklets, wearing gem-studded bracelets and arm-ornaments — the teacher of the universe.

[3]
I bow to Krishna, whose curly locks frame his face, whose countenance shines like the full moon, and who wears resplendent ear-rings — the teacher of the universe.

[4]
I bow to Krishna, who is fragrant with mandara flowers, whose smile is captivating, who has four arms, and whose crown is adorned with a peacock feather — the teacher of the universe.

[5]
I bow to Krishna, whose eyes are like the petals of a fully bloomed lotus, whose complexion resembles a dark rain-cloud, and who is the crown-jewel among the Yadava clan — the teacher of the universe.

[6]
I bow to Krishna, who sports with Rukmini, who is resplendent in yellow silk garments, and who is ever fragrant with the scent of tulasi — the teacher of the universe.

[7]
I bow to Krishna, whose broad chest bears the saffron marks from the bosoms of the gopis, who is the abode of Sri (Lakshmi), and who wields the great bow — the teacher of the universe.

[8]
I bow to Krishna, who bears the Srivatsa mark on his chest, who has a broad and mighty torso, who is adorned with a forest garland, and who holds the conch and discus — the divine teacher of the universe.

[9]
Whoever recites this sacred Krishnashtakam upon rising in the morning, all sins accumulated over crores of births are destroyed by that very remembrance.
